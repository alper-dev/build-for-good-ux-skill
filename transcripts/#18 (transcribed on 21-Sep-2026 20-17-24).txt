What do you think of disabled buttons? This is part 18 of my Build for Good UX series. The reason this button is faded is to tell you that it cannot be clicked, whether it's unavailable or something else needs to happen. But it turns out that these buttons aren't always the best user experience. Because when you can't continue, it's often on you as the user to figure out why. For example, if you complete a long form and the submit button is still disabled, you might think, I've already filled everything out. Now you need to search the entire form to see what you missed, especially when some fields are required and others aren't. Instead, if you're developing this kind of experience, you could leave the submit enabled. If the user clicks it with missing information, show a clear message, highlight the missing fields, and most importantly, take them directly to those so they don't have to scroll and try to find it. Disabled buttons can also create an accessibility issue. These buttons are usually skipped when someone is navigating using a keyboard or a screen reader, making the button and what enables it more difficult to discover. However, this doesn't mean the disabled buttons are always bad. They make sense when you click a button and you're waiting for something to happen. Because here, they can prevent duplicate submissions. They can also work when the reason is super obvious. Like this previous button being disabled on the first page or in a short survey with one required question at a time. So if you are wondering, do I add it or not? Then ask yourself, if I do, then will the user have to figure out how to enable the button? Or is it super obvious? And if it's not obvious, then probably don't disable it. Follow for part 19.
