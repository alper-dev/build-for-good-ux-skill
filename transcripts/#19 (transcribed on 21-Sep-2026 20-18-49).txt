Now look at the placement of Instagram's like, comment, and share buttons. These icons look pretty small and close together, but they are spaced far enough apart to where you usually select what you meant. There is something called Fitts' Law in UX. It says that larger targets and targets closer to where your finger is already located are faster and easier to select. On our phones, this is one of the reasons why the navigation is usually at the bottom. Because when you're holding your phone, where is your thumb? Most likely it is somewhere near the bottom of the screen, but a button or an icon doesn't always need to look bigger. Sometimes the area that responds to your tap is a little larger than the icon. For example, this is an icon, but there can also be this invisible padding around it that is also clickable. Another example is this checkbox. You could make the label and the checkbox clickable, or if an entire card leads to one place, you could make the entire card clickable. With this last example, you need to be careful because it can create a different problem. When someone is scrolling, the app needs to recognize that as a scroll, not accidentally tapping a card. Otherwise, you make one action easier while creating frustration somewhere else. See if you're testing your app on a physical device and you constantly miss the button.
