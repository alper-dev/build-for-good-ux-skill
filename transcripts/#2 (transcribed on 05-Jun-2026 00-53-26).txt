In 1984 the Mac showed you a tiny wristwatch cursor when something was loading. No animation. Windows came along in 1885 with the Hourglass, still no animation, and that lasted for about 20 years. And in the late 80s we started seeing progress bars for the first time. Research the time showed that 86% of people preferred them even if they weren't even accurate. In 2001, Mac OS X gave us the spinning rainbow wheel which I like to call the beach ball of death. It was animated, but it told you nothing. Has it been five seconds or two minutes? No idea. And technically, it still exists today, you just don't see it as much because the hardware is a lot better. Now we have skeleton screens, those gray placeholder outlines on Instagram or other apps. The concept came from a designer in 2013, Facebook was early to adopt it, and they work because your brain starts processing all of the layout before the data arrives. We do so use benders to inside buttons or in smaller parts of the page where the skeleton doesn't really make sense.
